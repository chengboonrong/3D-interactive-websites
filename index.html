A history
in plastic

Fifteen machines, 1989 to 2025, built from rounded slabs and cylinders and solved by your GPU as you scroll. Two product lines running in parallel for twenty-eight years, then one. Not a photograph among them.

Scroll

1989 · Handheld

Game Boy

A 90 mm slab with a four-shade olive LCD and no backlight. Nintendo counts it together with the Game Boy Color: 118.69 million between them.

1990 · 16-bit

Super Famicom

49.10 million sold worldwide as the Super Famicom and the SNES. Two hundred millimetres of grey ABS with a purple slider, and a shoulder curve nobody has really improved on.

1996 · 64-bit

Nintendo 64

32.93 million sold. Four controller ports as standard when everyone else shipped two, and an analogue stick in the box before anyone was asking for one.

1998 · Colour

Game Boy Color

Colour at last, and it still played the 1989 cartridges. Narrower, squarer, and the d-pad finally sat in a round cradle.

2001 · Landscape

Game Boy Advance

81.51 million across the whole Advance family. The first one you held sideways, with shoulder buttons wrapped over the top edge and a screen three times the area.

2001 · Optical

GameCube

21.74 million sold. A 150 mm cube with a carrying handle moulded into the back — the only home console Nintendo has ever asked you to pick up and take somewhere.

2003 · Folded

Game Boy Advance SP

The same screen as two years earlier, front-lit and folded in half. Eighty-two millimetres square, with the first rechargeable battery in the line. Counted inside the Advance family's 81.51 million.

2004 · Two screens

Nintendo DS

154.02 million sold, the best-selling handheld ever made. Two screens, the lower one a touchscreen, in a hinge that shut like a compact.

2006 · Refined

Nintendo DS Lite

The same two screens in a shell a fifth narrower and far brighter. It is the version most people picture, and it is counted inside the DS family's 154.02 million.

2006 · Motion

Wii

101.63 million sold, more than any other console of its generation. Also the smallest home console in this lineup: 44 mm thick, standing on a cradle.

2011 · Stereoscopic

Nintendo 3DS

75.94 million across the whole 3DS family. A widescreen top panel showing two images at once for depth without glasses, and a Circle Pad above the d-pad.

2012 · Second screen

Wii U

13.56 million sold — the fewest of any Nintendo home console. The idea was a decade early and the tablet was tethered to the living room.

2014 · Revised

New Nintendo 3DS

A C-Stick, two more shoulder buttons, and cameras that track your head so the 3D survives you moving. The face buttons went back to the Super Famicom's four colours.

2017 · One line

Nintendo Switch

Over 150 million sold. Twenty-eight years of two separate families end here: the dock stopped being a console and became a stand, and the handheld became the console.

2025 · Hybrid

Nintendo Switch 2

Launched 5 June 2025. Two hundred and seventy-two millimetres across the rails — 33 mm wider than the original — with the colour moved off the shells and onto the inner edge.

One scale, no assets

Thirty-six years, 150 KB

Everything here is drawn at one scale, 1 unit to 40 mm, so the sizes are true against each other — a Game Boy really is a fifth the width of a Nintendo 64. Where something is small, the camera steps closer rather than the model growing. Nothing was downloaded to draw any of it.
